What the internship is

You work on cases, not coffee.

Each group takes a stage of a federal case and builds something the firm actually uses — intake frameworks, evidence and spoliation practice, claims analysis, demand strategy, a pleadings bank. You work directly with Gina Dennis, and you leave with something real to talk about in an interview.

Summer 2026 · Group one

Intake, evidence, and multi-state jurisdiction.

The early stages of a federal trucking or tort-injury case: evaluating intake, preserving evidence, and working out which court hears a crash that crossed state lines.

  • Elizabeth Conley American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Builds intake frameworks for federal trucking accident and tort-injury cases — case evaluation, documentation requirements, and workflow. Previously with a Legal Aid Society, running client intake interviews and helping people navigate legal and social services.

  • Allison Guenther American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Works on evidence preservation and spoliation, with a focus on the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Regulations and litigation-ready evidentiary frameworks. Grew up around truck drivers in the extended family, and came to the work already understanding the industry.

  • Carter Hurley American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Researches multi-state trucking litigation in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Mexico, including FMCSA regulations and jurisdictional questions in interstate cases. Previously reviewed medical malpractice claims and served as a Guardian ad Litem in the North Carolina Judicial Branch.

Summer 2026 · Group two

Claims development and federal liability.

Federal claims involving trucking, environmental and hazardous materials exposure, and tort injury — including regulation under the FMCSA and liability under the Federal Tort Claims Act.

  • W. Grant O’Neal American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Researches federal claims involving private motor carriers and government entities — trucking accidents, environmental exposure, and Federal Tort Claims Act questions in interstate litigation. Brings experience in distribution operations: truck receiving, inventory, warehouse organisation.

  • Kaamyar Montazami American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Analyses federal trucking accident and tort-injury claims, including FMCSA regulatory issues and matters involving hazardous materials exposure in a cross-border context. Has unloaded food deliveries from commercial trucks, and knows the logistics side firsthand. Also an amateur boxer.

  • Elizabeth Johns American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Researches trucking, tort-injury, and environmental exposure claims, including how criminal liability concepts can inform civil litigation strategy. Volunteered at a tax clinic hosted by DNA People’s Legal Services for the Navajo Nation, and has a close family connection to a commercial truck driver.

Summer 2026 · Group three

Demand strategy, settlement, and pleadings.

How a case is valued and resolved: demand and settlement strategy, and a structured pleadings resource built from federal filing patterns in the District of New Mexico.

  • Madeleine Zoeller American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Develops demand and settlement frameworks for federal trucking and tort-injury cases, including how FMCSA violations bear on liability and settlement value. Worked in the Texas Senate on legislation to make the transport of recycled batteries safer for workers and the public.

  • Zena Sawaged American University · Washington College of Law · J.D. candidate

    Builds a federal pleadings bank by analysing filing patterns in trucking, environmental exposure, and tort-injury cases in the District of New Mexico.
